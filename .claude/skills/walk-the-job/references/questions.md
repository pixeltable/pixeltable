# The four questions

A good product solves the problem this person arrived with. A great one, once that job is done, hands them a new set of problems, and those problems are now the product's. The session names both.

Ask these on a brief. Read them before a walk, a research pass, and a critic pass, so those commands collect what the brief will need.

An answer you can point at is an answer. An open question is a result. A confident sentence with nothing behind it is a finding.

## Customers

- Do I truly understand the customer?
- Are there enough of them?

Understand: the person and the situation on the card, taken from the product. If the product names no one, this question is open.

Enough: a Fact in the research table. The product cannot answer it. If research has not been run, the question stays open. A guess about a market is not a Fact.

## Problem

- Does the problem seem truly painful?
- Have I described it in enough detail?
- Am I confident I know how to fix it?

Painful: what they do when they would leave, from a step, in the product's words.

Detail: the path and the steps name a screen, a command, or a page. A problem with no place is not described yet.

Fix: a change that names the place and the words. A row marked Check first means the fix is not known yet. Say that.

## Solution

- Does the solution really address the problem?
- Will customers love it?
- Am I focused on what's most important?

Addresses: Done is reachable, and the confirmation is in the product's words. If they cannot tell it worked, the solution has not addressed the job.

Love: they do the job a second time, or they take on a new problem the product created because the job was worth finishing. Write what they did. A sentence that they will love it, with no second time and no new problem, is not an answer.

Focused: one change, the one that blocks Done. Name what you deferred, and why it can wait.

## Implementation

- Are there any areas I am not certain how this must work?
- Are there features missing or features I should defer?

Uncertain: Check first rows, critic findings, and any step where the behavior could not be found in the product.

Missing: what the session needed and did not find, named by screen.

Defer: what can wait because it does not block Done. A missing piece that blocks Done is the change, and it stays in the change section.

## Problems this creates

After they succeed, list the new problems the product handed them. For each one, say whether the product already helps, in its own words, or whether the problem is open. If the session stopped before success, say the session has not reached it.
