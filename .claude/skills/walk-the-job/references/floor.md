# Floor

Read this before the steps. Each pair shows a writeup that looks finished, then the same moment written so a teammate can point at it.

The person and the labels below are illustrations. Leave them here.

## A sentence in their mouth

Breaks: "She clicks Get started and feels confident the setup will be quick."

The product never showed those words, and the feeling is yours. She cannot follow either one.

Holds: "The first screen's only button is `Create a desk`. She clicks it. The next screen is `Name`, an empty field, and `Continue`."

## One person

Breaks: "Users will be confused by the navigation."

A group has no single job, so the note has no single place the job stops.

Holds: "Rae is trying to export yesterday's notes. The header has `Search` and `New`. This screen never says export."

## A change with no place

Breaks: "Improve the empty state so it is more helpful."

Holds: "On `Notes`, the empty screen shows `No notes yet`. Under it, add `Write the first note` and make those words the button. `New` is in the header, and Rae did not connect it with the empty list."

## A second checklist beside the steps

Breaks: a section of visibility, consistency, and error prevention, with no step under it.

The four questions already ask whether they can see the action, connect it with the result, and tell that it worked. A second list buries the step where the job stopped.

Holds: the break stays in the step where it happened. The table points at that step.

## Praise

Breaks: "The visual design is clean and modern, which builds trust."

The session records where the job breaks. A compliment gives the next reader nothing to change.

Holds: "The title is `Inbox`. The first row is `Unread`. Rae can tell what this screen is for."

## A fix made during the walk

Breaks: the writeup includes a diff, and the table describes the product after your edit.

Holds: the table names the change. The product stays as you found it until they pick rows.

## The arrival problem, and then silence

Breaks: "This solves the problem they came with."

They arrived with a job. Finishing it hands them the next one. A writeup that stops at the arrival has not watched them succeed.

Holds: "They came to send the note. After `Send`, the screen is `Sent`. The new problem is whether the other person got it. The screen never says. That problem is open."

## Trouble skipped

Breaks: the happy path, then an empty table.

Holds: two troubles you produced, or a line that names the troubles you looked for, what you tried, and why the product could not produce them.
