# Walk the job

Follow one person while they attempt one job. The words they can act on must come from the product. Keep proposed behavior and observed behavior distinct. Read the job guide linked from the skill entry point before establishing or resuming the card.

## Commands

| Command | Reach |
| --- | --- |
| `cast` | Establish the job card, then stop |
| `walk` | Follow the job from arrival to completion or abandonment |
| `trouble` | Examine relevant empty, wrong, waiting, closed, return, or undo paths |
| `research` | Source facts outside the product on which this job depends |
| `critic` | Challenge an existing session or decision without rewriting it |
| `brief` | Recommend a decision from the card and evidence already collected |

A product review defaults to `walk`. A focused stage stays focused. Use the decisions guide linked from the entry point for research, critic, and brief. A plan can support a prospective walkthrough; mark its steps as proposed and its untested assumptions as open.

## Follow the product

Walk the stages the product has: arrival, entry, first success, repeat use, relevant trouble, leaving, and return. Name stages outside the reach or absent from the product. On a full session, attempt two relevant trouble paths when possible, or state what was attempted and why they could not be checked. Do not manufacture damage or effects outside the user's authorization to fill a quota.

At each step, ask whether the person can identify the next action, find it, connect it to the desired result, and tell whether it worked. Write what is visible, what they do, and what the product returns. Quote labels, commands, output, errors, and confirmation, with a screen, file, or command location. Missing words or confirmation are findings too.

Use the actual kind of arrival. Do not invent setup before a landing-page decision or call documentation successful before checking its example. On repeated work, examine what must be entered again and whether that repetition is needed.

After success, identify the new work created: finding the result, checking delivery, tracking progress, retrying, undoing, exporting, or cleanup. Say whether the product helps in its own words or leaves that problem open. When success was not reached, say so.

Keep changes tied to the place where the job breaks. Specify what to add, move, or remove. Drop generic advice that would fit another product unchanged. A product design choice is not automatically a bug; explain the cue or tradeoff the person needs.

## Write and check the session

Give the card, what was checked and how, the chronological steps, then the breaks:

| Step and location | What was needed | What the product did | Proposed change | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Distinguish a wording change, a flow change, and a question requiring a check. Keep evidence separate from inferred consequences. The walkthrough is a scenario evaluated against the product, not a claim that an actual customer acted or felt this way.

Read the draft back against the artifact. Verify every quote and location. Each proposed change points to that same place. A full session includes trouble and, after success, the resulting new work. An empty findings table without those checks does not establish that the experience holds.

Leave the product as found during review. If fixes were requested, apply the existing authorization and verify the affected steps. Do not insert a new approval stop. Otherwise present the proposed changes without implementing them.

Return the session in the response. When persistent output is requested, use the chosen location or `lifecycle/session.md`. Reuse existing notes for a follow-up; reread and recheck the steps affected by a change.
